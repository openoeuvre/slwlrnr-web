# SLWLRNR Activity Cards — working brief

Twenty-five card concepts with materials, steps, and the principle each one
teaches. Drafted during the SLWLRNR website build. **Not final copy** — these are
developed idea seeds, meant to be revised, cut, and expanded.

## Context

**SLWLRNR** (slwlrnr.art) is an art studio with three strands:

1. **Gallery** — original artworks sold as archival prints, mounted or rolled.
2. **Learn** — these activity cards.
3. **Create** — a generative tool that recombines source images through the same
   natural systems the cards teach.

The cards are the bridge. They teach, in physical materials, the processes the
generative tool performs computationally. Somebody who does the reaction–diffusion
card with two inks and a wet sheet understands what the software is doing.

### Language rules

- Say **computational collage** and **algorithmic transformation**. Never
  **AI art** — this is a deliberate positioning decision.
- Voice is plain, concrete, unhurried. Instructions are imperative and specific.
  No exclamation marks, no enthusiasm-by-punctuation.
- Every card ends on a principle: one sentence naming what the activity actually
  taught. That is the payload; the activity is the delivery mechanism.

### Commercial model (it shapes the cards)

- **First 5 are free**, so they must stand alone and earn the subscription.
- **$10/month** unlocks the full library plus print-ready PDFs.
- **A new card every other week**, so the format has to be repeatable.
- Order is meaningful — it is both the free/paid boundary and a difficulty ramp.

### Card format

Each card carries: number, title, natural system, working time, level
(Foundation / Intermediate / Open), a one-line summary, a materials list, three
or four numbered steps, and the principle.

## The natural systems registry

Every card is tied to one of these. They are the same systems offered in the
generative tool, which is what makes the cards and the software one product.

**Signature** — The house blend. Balanced, legible, forgiving of most sources.

> Our default transformation. It reads the structure of both sources and negotiates between them without letting either dominate. Start here.

**Reaction–Diffusion** — Turing patterns. Spots, stripes, and the boundaries between them.

> Two competing agents spread across the surface at different rates. Where they meet, they organise into the same families of pattern that appear on shells, pelts, and coral.

**Fluid Dynamics** — Advection and vorticity. Sources dragged through a velocity field.

> Your images are treated as dye in a moving medium. Edges become streaklines; flat regions become laminar sheets. Strong sources hold their shape longest.

**Dendritic** — Branching growth. River networks, lightning, frost on glass.

> Growth propagates outward along paths of least resistance, splitting as it goes. Produces the recursive branching common to drainage basins and electrical discharge.

**Cellular / Voronoi** — Partitioned space. Every point belongs to its nearest seed.

> The surface is divided into cells around scattered seed points. Reads as cracked glaze, dragonfly wing, or the packing of soap foam depending on seed density.

**Phyllotaxis** — Spiral packing at the golden angle. Sunflowers and pinecones.

> Elements are placed on a spiral at roughly 137.5°, the arrangement plants use to pack seeds without gaps. Produces interlocking parastichy spirals.

**Mycelial** — Network foraging. Threads that seek, connect, and thicken.

> A network explores the surface, reinforcing routes that find something and abandoning those that do not. The result is an anastomosing web with visible traffic.

**Cymatic Resonance** — Standing waves. Chladni figures and nodal geometry.

> The surface is driven at frequency until material collects along the nodal lines where it is still. Symmetry emerges from vibration rather than from drawing.

**Ferrofluid Fields** — Magnetic spiking. Surface tension losing to field strength.

> A field pulls the surface into ordered peaks — the Rosensweig instability. Produces dense, glossy, spiked topography with hard specular edges.

**Mineral Banding** — Deposition in layers. Agate, malachite, sedimentary rock.

> Material accretes in successive bands that record the conditions at the moment they formed. Reads as geological section or cut and polished stone.

## The twenty-five cards

### 01 · Two Inks, One Sheet

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Reaction–Diffusion |
| **Time** | 30 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Free |

Watch two liquids compete for the same surface and settle into a pattern neither one chose.

**You will need:** Two ink colours · Heavy paper · Pipette or dropper · Water

**Steps**

1. Wet the sheet unevenly — leave some areas dry.
2. Drop the first ink at four points and let it find its edges.
3. Introduce the second ink where the first has already spread.
4. Do not intervene. Photograph the boundary at five and fifteen minutes.

**What this teaches:** Pattern can emerge from two simple rules interacting, without anyone deciding what the pattern should be.

### 02 · Finding the Branch Point

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Dendritic |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Free |

Trace a real branching system and discover where it decides to split.

**You will need:** Tracing paper · Fine pen · A photograph of a river, tree, or crack

**Steps**

1. Trace only the thickest line from source to end.
2. Add the second-thickest lines. Mark each split with a dot.
3. Add a third level. Notice how the angles repeat.
4. Measure three split angles. Write them on the sheet.

**What this teaches:** Branching systems reuse the same decision at every scale. Recognising that repetition is the whole skill.

---

### 03 · The Sunflower Count

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Phyllotaxis |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Free |

Count the spirals in a seed head and find the numbers hiding in them.

**You will need:** Sunflower head, pinecone, or a printed photograph · Coloured pencils

**Steps**

1. Pick one spiral running clockwise and trace it to the edge.
2. Colour every clockwise spiral. Count them.
3. Repeat anticlockwise in a second colour. Count those.
4. Compare your two numbers to the sequence 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34.

**What this teaches:** Efficient packing produces Fibonacci counts as a side effect. The plant is not counting; the geometry is.

---

### 04 · Salt on a Drum

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Cymatic Resonance |
| **Time** | 30 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Free |

Make a vibrating surface show you where it is standing still.

**You will need:** Cling film and a bowl · Fine salt · A speaker or tone app

**Steps**

1. Stretch the film tight over the bowl and scatter salt evenly.
2. Play a low tone nearby and raise the frequency slowly.
3. Stop whenever the salt organises. Note the frequency.
4. Find three different frequencies that produce three different figures.

**What this teaches:** Symmetry here comes from vibration, not from drawing. The pattern marks the parts that are not moving.

### 05 · Nearest Neighbour

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Cellular / Voronoi |
| **Time** | 35 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Free |

Divide a page so that every point belongs to whichever dot is closest.

**You will need:** Paper · Ruler · Pencil

**Steps**

1. Mark eight dots at random across the sheet.
2. For each pair of nearby dots, draw the line exactly halfway between them.
3. Keep only the segments that are closest to both of their dots.
4. Shade each finished region a different value.

**What this teaches:** One rule — belong to the nearest seed — generates cracked glaze, giraffe hide, and city catchment maps alike.

### 06 · Dye in Moving Water

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Fluid Dynamics |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Learn to read a flow by what it does to a colour dropped into it.

**You will need:** Shallow tray · Water · Food colouring · Straw

**Steps**

1. Fill the tray and let the water go completely still.
2. Place one drop of colour and photograph it every ten seconds.
3. Introduce a slow current with the straw from one edge only.
4. Record how long the drop keeps a recognisable edge.

**What this teaches:** A flow is invisible until something is carried by it. Artists borrow this constantly — the mark reveals the force.

---

### 07 · Growing Toward Food

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Mycelial |
| **Time** | 2 weeks |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Set up a foraging network and watch it abandon its own failed routes.

**You will need:** Agar plate or damp cardboard · Oat flakes · Sealed container

**Steps**

1. Place three food sources at the corners of a triangle.
2. Introduce growth at the centre and seal the container.
3. Photograph from directly above every second day.
4. Mark which routes thicken and which disappear.

**What this teaches:** The final network is a record of decisions, not a plan. Reinforcement and abandonment do the designing.

---

### 08 · Bands of Time

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Mineral Banding |
| **Time** | 1 week |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Build a layered deposit and read your own record back out of it.

**You will need:** Tall clear jar · Sand, soil, chalk, coffee grounds · Water

**Steps**

1. Add one thin layer each day, varying the material.
2. Keep a written log of what you added and when.
3. After a week, look at the jar without the log and try to reconstruct it.
4. Note which layers are legible and which are ambiguous.

**What this teaches:** Every band records the conditions of its own moment. Geology is a document you learn to read.

### 09 · Two Sources, One Image

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Signature |
| **Time** | 50 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Combine two of your own photographs by hand before ever using the tool.

**You will need:** Two printed photographs · Scalpel · Glue stick · Backing board

**Steps**

1. Choose one image for structure and one for surface.
2. Cut the structure image into strips of uneven width.
3. Interleave with strips from the second and fix them down.
4. Step back three metres. Decide which image is winning.

**What this teaches:** Blending is negotiation, not averaging. Deciding what each source contributes is the actual creative act.

### 10 · Spikes and Spacing

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Ferrofluid Fields |
| **Time** | 35 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Find out what sets the distance between peaks in a driven surface.

**You will need:** Iron filings · Sheet of acetate · Strong magnet

**Steps**

1. Scatter filings evenly and hold the acetate flat.
2. Bring the magnet slowly up underneath and stop when peaks form.
3. Photograph from a low angle and measure peak-to-peak spacing.
4. Repeat at a greater distance and compare the spacing.

**What this teaches:** Regular spacing emerges from two forces in competition. Neither one is choosing the interval.

### 11 · Reading a Crack

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Cellular / Voronoi |
| **Time** | 30 min |
| **Level** | Foundation |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Work out the order in which a cracked surface actually broke.

**You will need:** Photograph of dried mud, old paint, or crazed glaze · Marker

**Steps**

1. Find a junction where one crack stops at another.
2. The one that stops is younger. Mark it with a 2.
3. Work outward, numbering by relative age.
4. Reconstruct the full sequence from your numbers.

**What this teaches:** A static pattern can encode its own history. Learning to read sequence out of stillness transfers everywhere.

### 12 · The Angle That Never Repeats

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Phyllotaxis |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Test why 137.5° packs a surface better than any simpler angle.

**You will need:** Protractor · Compass · Three sheets of paper

**Steps**

1. On sheet one, place 40 dots turning 90° and stepping outward each time.
2. On sheet two, use 120°. On sheet three, use 137.5°.
3. Compare the gaps in each.
4. Explain in one sentence why the third fills best.

**What this teaches:** An angle that never divides evenly into a full turn never lets points fall into line — so nothing overlaps.

### 13 · Slowing a Reaction Down

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Reaction–Diffusion |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Change one rate and watch spots turn into stripes.

**You will need:** Two inks · Gelatine sheets · Warm and cold water

**Steps**

1. Prepare two gelatine surfaces, one thin and one thick.
2. Apply the same ink pair to both in the same arrangement.
3. Photograph both at equal intervals.
4. Describe how the pattern differs and which rate changed.

**What this teaches:** Spots and stripes are the same system at different speed ratios. One parameter changes the entire vocabulary.

### 14 · Streaklines

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Fluid Dynamics |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Build a flow with an obstacle in it and record what forms behind.

**You will need:** Tray · Water · Ink · Small stone or cork

**Steps**

1. Establish a steady current down the length of the tray.
2. Place the obstacle a third of the way along.
3. Release ink upstream and photograph the wake.
4. Sketch the two rotating regions behind the obstacle.

**What this teaches:** Obstacles create structure downstream. In composition, the interruption is often more interesting than the flow.

### 15 · Same Rule, Four Scales

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Dendritic |
| **Time** | 50 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Draw one branching rule at four magnifications and compare.

**You will need:** Four sheets · Fine and broad pens · Ruler

**Steps**

1. Write down a rule: split at 40°, each branch 0.65 as long.
2. Apply it four times on sheet one, six on sheet two, eight on sheet three.
3. On sheet four, apply it eight times but vary each angle by ±10°.
4. Compare the fourth to a photograph of a real tree.

**What this teaches:** Real systems follow rules imperfectly. The variation is what makes the result read as alive.

### 16 · What the Surface Kept

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Signature |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Blend two images and account for exactly what survived from each.

**You will need:** Two of your own images · Notebook

**Steps**

1. Before blending, list five features you expect to survive from each source.
2. Produce the blend.
3. Tick the features that survived; strike those that did not.
4. Write one sentence on why the survivors survived.

**What this teaches:** Prediction then comparison is how you build intuition for a system faster than repetition alone.

### 17 · Nodal Lines by Hand

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Cymatic Resonance |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Draw standing waves and find where they cancel.

**You will need:** Graph paper · Two coloured pencils

**Steps**

1. Draw a wave across the sheet, then the same wave reversed.
2. At every grid line, add the two heights together.
3. Plot the sum. Mark every point where it reaches zero.
4. Compare the spacing of your zeros to the wavelength.

**What this teaches:** Interference produces fixed points from pure motion. Those fixed points are what a cymatic figure shows.

### 18 · Cost of a Connection

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Mycelial |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Design a network three ways and work out which one a forager would build.

**You will need:** Paper · String · Ruler

**Steps**

1. Mark six points. Connect every pair. Measure total length.
2. Connect them as a single chain. Measure again.
3. Now find the shortest network that still connects all six.
4. Compare all three against a photograph of a real network.

**What this teaches:** Living networks trade total cost against resilience. Neither extreme is what evolution actually picks.

### 19 · Unconformity

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Mineral Banding |
| **Time** | 1 week |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Build a layered record with a gap in it and see if the gap reads.

**You will need:** Clear jar · Layer materials · Spoon

**Steps**

1. Deposit five layers over five days.
2. On day six, remove the top two layers with the spoon.
3. Deposit three more layers on the disturbed surface.
4. Ask someone else to find the missing time.

**What this teaches:** Absence is legible. What was removed leaves a boundary as distinct as what remains.

### 20 · Seeds and Density

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Cellular / Voronoi |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Change only seed density and watch the character of a pattern shift.

**You will need:** Three sheets · Pencil · Ruler

**Steps**

1. Partition sheet one around five seeds, sheet two around twenty, sheet three around sixty.
2. Use the same method each time.
3. Name what each one resembles.
4. Note the density at which it stops reading as structure and starts reading as texture.

**What this teaches:** Quantity changes meaning. The same rule at a different density becomes a different image entirely.

### 21 · Choosing the Dominant Source

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Signature |
| **Time** | 50 min |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Run the same pair of images at five weightings and find where it turns.

**You will need:** Two source images · Notebook

**Steps**

1. Blend at 90/10, 70/30, 50/50, 30/70, 10/90.
2. Lay all five out in order.
3. Mark the point where the reading flips from one source to the other.
4. Note that it is rarely at 50/50.

**What this teaches:** Perceptual balance is not numerical balance. Structure outweighs surface almost every time.

### 22 · Instability Threshold

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Ferrofluid Fields |
| **Time** | 40 min |
| **Level** | Intermediate |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Find the exact moment a smooth surface gives way to structure.

**You will need:** Iron filings · Acetate · Magnet · Ruler

**Steps**

1. Start with the magnet far enough away that nothing happens.
2. Move it closer in five-millimetre steps.
3. Record the distance at which peaks first appear.
4. Repeat three times and see how repeatable the threshold is.

**What this teaches:** Many systems do nothing at all until a threshold, then reorganise completely. Change is not always gradual.

### 23 · Deliberate Interruption

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Fluid Dynamics |
| **Time** | 45 min |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Use an obstacle as a compositional decision rather than an accident.

**You will need:** Tray · Water · Ink · Three different obstacles

**Steps**

1. Run the same flow past each obstacle in turn.
2. Photograph each wake from the same position.
3. Choose the one you would build a composition around.
4. Write down why in one sentence.

**What this teaches:** Placing the obstacle is the composition. In generative work, constraint is the main instrument you hold.

### 24 · One System, Two Sources

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Signature |
| **Time** | 1 hour |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Hold the system constant and vary only the inputs, five times over.

**You will need:** Ten source images · Notebook

**Steps**

1. Fix one generative system and do not change it.
2. Run five different pairs through it.
3. Lay the five results out together.
4. Describe in one sentence what the system does regardless of input.

**What this teaches:** Every system has a signature that survives its inputs. Knowing it is how you choose deliberately.

### 25 · Building a Series

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **System** | Signature |
| **Time** | 2 hours |
| **Level** | Open |
| **Access** | Subscribers |

Assemble six related works and decide what actually makes them a set.

**You will need:** Six of your own blends · Wall space or a large table

**Steps**

1. Lay all six out and remove the weakest.
2. Name the through-line in five words or fewer.
3. Replace the removed work with one that strengthens that line.
4. Order the six for a wall, then reorder them once more.

**What this teaches:** A series is an argument. Editing and sequencing carry as much of it as any single image does.

## Known gaps — read before building

- **None of this has been tested.** The activities are plausible and grounded in
  real phenomena, but nobody has run them. Timings, quantities, and success rates
  are estimates. Card 07 (mycelial growth, two weeks) and card 19 (unconformity,
  one week) need the most validation.
- **No safety guidance anywhere.** Several cards involve inks, a scalpel, magnets,
  or growing cultures. Age-appropriateness has not been assessed.
- **No visual design.** No layout, format, or physical card dimensions decided.
- **Card 25 assumes prior output** from the generative tool, so it cannot be
  attempted by someone who only bought the cards.
- **Systems are unevenly covered.** Signature and the fluid/branching systems
  recur; others appear once. Worth rebalancing if the library grows.
